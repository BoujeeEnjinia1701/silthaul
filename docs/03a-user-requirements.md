# SiltHaul user requirements

What users need, written from their side. The thirteen needs below were drafted from the users and stakeholders in SLH-PRB-001 (Tables 1 and 2), used to score the concept options (SLH-OPT-001), and then traced to the product requirements in SLH-PRS-001 (SLH-RTM-001). The twelve original requirements R1 to R12 that Amish accepted on 2026-10-03 are kept as product requirements PR-001 to PR-012; nothing Amish decided was changed. The needs remain a desk draft until a co-design partner reviews them with the people who clear flood mud. Method: STANDARDS section 22.

Table 1. User requirements.

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Performance | Nobody lifts or carries mud | The mud goes out of the room without anyone lifting it or carrying it | High | Householders and volunteers (PRB Table 1); Amish's restatement of R1, SLH-DDR-003 |
| UR-002 | Performance | Keeps up with a bucket crew | A crew of four moves at least half as much mud an hour as the same crew with shovels and buckets | High | Householders and volunteers; relief NGOs |
| UR-003 | Usability | Ordinary volunteers can work it | A person of ordinary strength can work it after a short briefing, with no heavy single effort | High | Relief NGOs (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-004 | Deployment and assembly | Gets through our door | It works through a doorway about 0.7 m wide | High | Householders; PRB operating environment |
| UR-005 | Performance | Reaches the far end of the room | It clears a room up to 15 m from where the crew works | Medium | Municipal drain and sanitation crews |
| UR-006 | Safety | Safe near ropes, anchors and moving parts | Nobody is hurt by a rope that lets go, an anchor that fails, a load that runs back or a moving part | High | Relief NGOs; disaster authorities (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-007 | Deployment and assembly | Quick to set up | Three untrained volunteers set it up in 20 minutes after a short briefing | Medium | Relief NGOs |
| UR-008 | Logistics | Travels in a small car | The kit fits a small hatchback and no single part is more than 25 kg | Medium | Relief NGOs; PRB constraints |
| UR-009 | Cost | Affordable for a relief group | The prototype parts stay at or under the USD 2,000 value-engineering target | Medium | Relief NGOs; PRB constraints |
| UR-010 | Maintenance | Made and repaired locally | A local fabricator can make and mend it from stock steel, rope and commercial parts with hand tools and a small welder | High | Local fabricators (PRB Table 1) |
| UR-011 | Environment and durability | Survives mud and wet | It keeps working in wet mud and can be washed down | Medium | All users; PRB operating environment |
| UR-012 | Safety | Keeps people out of the mud | The crew spends less time standing in and touching contaminated mud, and the crank crew stays clear of it | High | Householders and volunteers; local health authorities |
| UR-013 | Environment and durability | Adds no wet waste | It moves the mud as it is, with no added water to dispose of | Low | Municipal drain and sanitation crews |
